In short: Smartnote is a desktop application that runs on your computer. Its author operates no servers and receives none of your data. Your notes are encrypted on your device. If you choose a remote storage, only encrypted data is sent there, directly from your device. Smartnote has no analytics, telemetry, crash reporting or ads.

Who this policy is about

This policy covers the Smartnote application published at github.com/nikchis/smartnote (“Smartnote”, “we”). It describes what the application does with your data. Smartnote is open-source software, so every statement here can be checked against its source code.

Data on your device

Smartnote stores the following in its local database on your computer:

  • your notes and their version history;
  • the vectors used for semantic search;
  • the sign-in tokens of a cloud storage, if you sign in to one.

These items are encrypted with a key protected by your password. Smartnote also stores its settings there, and writes a log file. The log never contains the text of your notes, your search queries, passwords, sync phrases or sign-in tokens. The search index exists only in memory while Smartnote runs.

None of this data is sent to the author of Smartnote or to anyone else.

Remote storage you choose

Remote storage is optional. By default Smartnote works only on your device. If you choose one, Smartnote sends your notes there to back them up and reads them back to restore them. The first connection to an empty storage makes the first backup automatically:

  • your own PostgreSQL server reachable over SSH;
  • Yandex Disk;
  • Google Drive.

Before the notes leave your device they are encrypted with a key derived from your sync phrase, which never leaves your device. The storage provider receives only ciphertext and cannot read your notes. The provider still sees what any file storage sees: your account, your IP address, when data is written, and the approximate size of the stored data. The provider's own privacy policy applies to that information.

Google user data

This section applies when you choose Google Drive as the storage and sign in with Google.

What Smartnote accesses

Smartnote requests only the https://www.googleapis.com/auth/drive.appdata scope. This gives access to Smartnote's own hidden application data folder on your Google Drive. It gives no access to any other file, folder or data in your Google account. In that folder Smartnote keeps one file: the encrypted container with your notes. If two of your devices create the storage at the same moment, a second copy of the container may stay there for a while; Smartnote removes it during a later backup.

How Smartnote uses it

Smartnote uses this access only to keep the encrypted backup of your notes. It writes the backup when you press Backup, and when it connects to an empty storage for the first time (the first backup is made automatically then, for example after you press OK in Settings). It reads the backup when it connects, when you test the connection in Settings, when it prepares or makes a backup and when you restore, including on your other devices. The data travels directly between your device and Google. It is not sent to the author of Smartnote or to any other party.

Storage and protection

The tokens issued by Google at sign-in are stored only on your device, encrypted in the local database with the key protected by your password. They are sent only to Google, over TLS. Smartnote never writes them to its log.

Sharing

Smartnote does not share, transfer, sell or disclose Google user data to anyone. It does not use it for advertising, and does not use it to develop, improve or train artificial intelligence or machine learning models. No person reads it. The author has no copy of it.

Limited Use

Smartnote's use and transfer of information received from Google APIs will adhere to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements.

Retention and deletion

  • Sign out in Smartnote settings, confirmed with OK, deletes the Google tokens from your device. The backup on your Drive stays, so you can restore it after signing in again.
  • To revoke Smartnote's access, remove it at myaccount.google.com/permissions.
  • To delete the backup, first choose another storage, or sign out, in Smartnote on every device that uses Google Drive: otherwise the next connection creates the backup again. Then open Google Drive settings, choose Manage apps, find Smartnote and choose Delete hidden app data.
  • Uninstalling Smartnote and deleting its data folder removes everything it stored on your device.

Yandex user data

When you choose Yandex Disk, Smartnote requests access only to its own application folder on your Disk. It uses this access, stores the tokens and keeps the data exactly as described above for Google. To revoke the access, use the list of applications with access in your Yandex ID settings. To delete the backup, first choose another storage, or sign out, in Smartnote on every device that uses Yandex Disk, then delete the file smartnote.v1.bin in the Smartnote application folder inside Applications on your Disk.

Network connections

Smartnote connects to the network only to reach the storage you configured, and to complete a sign-in, which happens in your web browser. It does not check for updates in the background, does not load remote content and does not contact the author. Links to documentation open in your browser only when you click them.

Children

Smartnote is a general-purpose tool and is not directed at children.
